# Object 01 — Product Use Cases

## 1. Purpose and positioning

Object 01 belongs first to the gathering. Secondary uses let the same visual language continue through the home: reflective steel, considered arrangements, and space around everyday objects.

This guide defines proposed uses, styling directions, example copy, and validation requirements. It supports product pages, photography, AI briefs, social content, and customer education. It is not evidence that every proposed application has been tested.

The primary audience is the Millennial Host who wants a distinctive, welcoming setting that works with existing belongings. Broader home uses should support that proposition without turning the product into a generic organizer.

**Positioning line:** One object. A place at the table. A presence beyond it.

Technical terms such as “plate,” “charger,” and “tray” appear here as internal descriptions. Published brand copy must follow `voice.md`, including its prohibited-word rules. Use Object 01, setting, surface, or an accurate approved category description in customer-facing text.

## 2. Evidence and release rules

The current `product.md` is a draft. Food-contact suitability, selected alloy, dimensions, care performance, and production release remain unverified. The nominal 280 mm diameter is a concept target, not a measured compatibility promise.

Use the following status system:

| Status | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Concept | May be explored in internal styling or clearly identified concept imagery. |
| Sample test required | Fit, stability, surface contact, or normal-use behavior must be checked on the actual object. |
| Product evidence required | The intended application needs the applicable released product and care evidence. |
| Released | Reserved for a use approved against a documented product revision. No use is marked released here. |

Example captions below are editorial drafts. Publishing an image can imply suitability even without an explicit claim. Release both the image and its associated copy against the same approved use.

## 4. Detailed use cases

### UC01 — Direct dining and eating

**Customer need:** A complete dinner setting with a distinctive material presence.

**Scenario:** A relaxed dinner for two or four. Object 01 serves as the individual eating surface.

**Styling:** Use a small serving of steak, mashed potato, and green beans, or a similarly composed vegetarian meal. Keep food appetizing and portions plausible. Leave a visible band of Mirror Polish around the composition. Place cutlery beside the object. A distant vase can establish a lived-in room without competing with the food.

**Light:** Grand Chandelier for an evening gathering. Architectural Day for a daylight meal.

**Draft caption:** “The evening settles around the table. Object 01 catches the light.”

**Validation:** Confirm finished-product suitability for the intended foods, temperatures, contact times, and cleaning conditions. Test normal cutlery interaction and disclose realistic surface wear. Do not promise scratch resistance or retained heat. A direct steak-cutting scene requires an honest understanding of knife marks.

**Offer fit:** The Host Set for four individual settings. The Duo for two.

### UC02 — Double-layered charger or presentation setting

**Customer need:** Add reflective contrast while retaining existing ceramic dinnerware.

**Scenario:** Object 01 sits underneath a smaller ceramic eating vessel. One Object 01 forms the reflective lower layer.

**Styling:** Centre an ivory or warm-white ceramic piece inside the basin. Keep its foot fully supported and a clear steel perimeter visible. Add restrained linen and adjacent cutlery. Show the side relationship in at least one practical photograph.

**Fit rule:** Measure the actual usable basin, rim transition, and ceramic foot. Outer diameter alone cannot establish fit. Reject combinations that bridge the bead, rock, slide, or hide the entire steel surface. Do not invent a universal compatible diameter.

**Light:** Grand Chandelier for discrete rim highlights; Gallery Halogen for a close detail.

**Draft caption:** “Ceramic meets Mirror Polish. Light gathers at the edge.”

**Validation:** Test the selected pairing on the real object, including movement and underside contact. Check for abrasive ceramic feet. Do not claim protective or non-slip performance without evidence.

**Offer fit:** One Object 01 per setting. The Host Set provides four lower layers, not eight pieces and not included ceramics. Two identical Object 01 pieces stacked together are not the default layered concept; nesting and stacking require separate approval.

### UC03 — Dessert presentation

**Customer need:** A memorable final course with minimal additional table styling.

**Scenario:** A small red-and-cream pudding or berry panna cotta placed within the reflective basin.

**Styling:** Keep the dessert compact. Use a controlled amount of red fruit sauce and a small garnish. Leave most of the rim and substantial basin area visible. Reflections should contain the dessert’s real colors and shape rather than invented luminous effects.

**Light:** Dawn Horizon for a morning editorial concept; Vesper Flame for an evening course.

**Draft caption:** “Cream, berries, and a quiet reflection. The final course arrives.”

**Validation:** Direct food contact requires approved evidence. Confirm care guidance for the actual ingredients. Avoid imagery that implies freezer, oven, or other temperature capabilities not approved for the product.

**Offer fit:** One piece for individual presentation; multiple pieces for individual courses.

### UC04 — Shared bites and small food service

**Customer need:** A considered centre-of-table arrangement for a small gathering.

**Scenario:** A modest arrangement of pastries, bread, or small bites. Wet accompaniments may sit in a separate stable vessel.

**Styling:** Choose one food family and a restrained arrangement. Keep an open crescent of steel. Show a serving utensil nearby where useful. Use a stationary table setting rather than a hand carrying several unstable items.

**Light:** Architectural Day for lunch; Vesper Flame for evening.

**Draft caption:** “Something to share. Light rests between each piece.”

**Validation:** Direct-contact foods require product evidence. Separate containers still need stable placement and compatible bases. Do not imply that the shallow basin is designed for deep liquids or that the rim functions as a carrying handle.

**Offer fit:** One piece may suit a small arrangement. Do not infer guest capacity from nominal diameter.

### UC05 — Perfume bottle display

**Customer need:** Give a few fragrance bottles a deliberate place on a dresser.

**Scenario:** A stationary composition of one to three closed bottles, adjusted to their actual footprints.

**Styling:** Use clear or amber glass with uncluttered forms. Leave visible space around the bases. Place Object 01 on a dry, level dresser away from the edge. Photograph the bottles as the use context while retaining the steel rim and reflected glass as the visual focus.

**Light:** Gallery Halogen or soft Architectural Day. Keep perfume out of a direct-sun styling setup; create the lighting effect without prolonged product exposure.

**Draft caption:** “Glass gathers on Mirror Polish. A place for the daily ritual.”

**Validation:** Test bottle stability and base contact. Keep containers closed. Do not spray fragrance onto the surface or market it as a chemical-resistant spill catcher. No leak-containment, stain-resistance, or protective-performance claim is established.

**Offer fit:** A single Object 01. Perfume bottles are not included.

### UC06 — Aesthetic jewellery display

**Customer need:** A visible resting place for a few pieces removed at the end of the day.

**Scenario:** A pair of earrings, a ring, and a gently laid chain on a bedside or dressing surface.

**Styling:** Arrange a few pieces with generous separation. Avoid a tangled heap. Preserve the circular silhouette and actual metal reflections. Place delicate or abrasive pieces on an appropriate soft insert if needed; disclose that accessory separately.

**Light:** Dawn Horizon for a quiet ritual or Gallery Halogen for a close study.

**Draft caption:** “The evening leaves a few small things. Object 01 holds their reflection.”

**Validation:** Inspect for snag points and test representative contact carefully. Do not claim anti-tarnish, scratch-proof, padded, secure, or archival storage properties. This is an open display concept, not a substitute for appropriate jewellery storage.

**Offer fit:** A single Object 01. Jewellery and any insert are not included.

### UC07 — Entryway landing surface

**Customer need:** A consistent place for a few belongings on arriving home.

**Scenario:** A cardholder and key set placed on a console.

**Styling:** Keep the composition sparse. Use an existing soft pouch beneath sharp keys when appropriate. Show real domestic scale and avoid presenting the object as a deep storage bowl.

**Light:** Architectural Day.

**Draft caption:** “The day pauses at the door. A place for the essentials.”

**Validation:** Keys and repeated contact may affect the finish. Assess surface wear and explain it clearly before featuring this as routine use. Check the console contact points. Do not claim that Object 01 protects furniture.

**Offer fit:** A single piece. Lower editorial priority than dining, perfume, or jewellery because the hard-contact use can conflict with expectations of an unmarked mirror surface.

### UC08 — Coffee or tea presentation

**Customer need:** A compact arrangement for a quiet drink or welcoming a guest.

**Scenario:** A cup on its own saucer, with a small wrapped accompaniment or an independently supported snack vessel.

**Styling:** Use a stable cup-and-saucer combination. Show the arrangement resting on a table. Keep enough exposed steel to reflect the cup and room. Do not make the rim appear to have handles.

**Light:** Dawn Horizon.

**Draft caption:** “A cup rests in morning light. The room is still.”

**Validation:** Test the full arrangement for fit, slipping, contact marks, and temperature effects. Do not imply insulation, heatproof performance, or suitability as a trivet. Direct food contact has its own release requirement.

**Offer fit:** One piece per arrangement. This brief does not authorize carrying hot drinks on the object.

### UC09 — Small vase and sculptural still life

**Customer need:** A restrained focal point for a dining or coffee table between gatherings.

**Scenario:** A small, stable vase with one branch or stem placed within Object 01, or a compact sculptural object with a compatible base.

**Styling:** Limit the composition to one main accent. Keep the base, exposed steel, and rim visible. Use a dry, clean vase exterior. Distinguish this arrangement from a background vase that sits directly on the table.

**Light:** Architectural Day or Gallery Halogen.

**Draft caption:** “A stem rises above the reflection. The centre remains quiet.”

**Validation:** Check the actual load, footprint, tipping behavior, and contact surface. Do not publish a maximum load without testing. This is not a planter, drainage saucer, or vessel for standing water. Open flames, burning incense, and hot candle containers are outside this approved concept scope.

**Offer fit:** A single Object 01. Vase and botanical styling are not included.

### UC10 — Host gift and recipient-selected use

**Customer need:** A considered housewarming or host gift with an understandable purpose.

**Scenario:** A single piece for a personal arrangement, The Duo for two settings, or The Host Set for a small gathering.

**Styling:** Present the actual supplied contents and packaging. A short use card can suggest a released table use and a released home-display use. Do not stage accessories inside the package in a way that implies inclusion.

**Light:** Grand Chandelier for a hosting story; Dawn Horizon for a new-home story.

**Draft caption:** “A place in their home. An object for their own rituals.”

**Validation:** Confirm current set contents, packaging, gift-note availability, and delivery information. No gift-ready, gift-wrap, handwritten-note, or arrival promise without operational confirmation.

**Offer fit:** Match quantity to the recipient’s intended use. Gifting is a purchase occasion, not an additional physical capability.

## 5. Cross-use boundaries

- Treat food use and personal-object display as distinct routines. Do not show perfume, keys, or jewellery sharing the surface with food.
- Before a change of use, follow the released cleaning guidance and inspect the surface. Do not promise that casual wiping makes every cross-use transition suitable.
- Do not use Object 01 as cookware, a cutting board, a hot-pan rest, a microwave accessory, or a chemical container without separate product approval.
- Do not imply dishwasher, oven, freezer, slip resistance, stacking, scratch resistance, or furniture-protection performance from the appearance of steel.
- Accessories must fit the actual support area and remain stable. Add no fictional handles, compartments, coatings, padded lining, or deeper basin.
- Practical utility should remain understandable. Atmospheric language must not replace use limitations or contents information.

## 6. Visual direction for use-case content

### Separate two asset families

**Specular studies:** Preserve the existing Global Anchor and fixed object-and-plinth composition. Light remains the sole narrative variable. No food or lifestyle props enter this controlled comparison series.

**Use-case editorials:** Explicitly identify these as a separate campaign family. They may use food, cutlery, ceramics, bottles, jewellery, and a contemporary interior. Maintain the approved reference silhouette, beaded rim, seamless basin, finish, and restrained material palette.

### Shared production rules

1. Use the approved product reference. Never change geometry to make a prop fit.
2. Start with a 4:5 hero, an 85 mm equivalent lens, 30° camera elevation, f/4, and fixed 4300 K white balance. Practical compatibility views may use a separate clearly briefed angle.
3. Keep Object 01 visually central. Allow depth of field to soften the room while leaving the object and intended use legible.
4. Show enough exposed steel to read the finish. As a creative target, aim for roughly half the basin visible in sparse display scenes; dining and ceramic layering may need more coverage. This is not a product specification.
5. Preserve physically plausible reflections. Food and props must occlude and reflect naturally. Never depict the steel as transparent, self-lit, or a flawless mirror detached from its environment.
6. Use charcoal, warm white, stone, wood, linen, clear glass, and restrained food colors. Let practical context feel domestic rather than showroom-like.
7. Keep source fixtures off-frame when following a named lighting archetype. Avoid graphic starbursts or added sparkle effects.
8. Use a clean prepared surface for production imagery while keeping any customer-facing wear guidance honest.

### Lighting assignments

| Study | Source and angle | Best use-case application |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Dawn Horizon | 2800 K soft sun, 15°–20° grazing | Dessert, jewellery, coffee ritual |
| Vesper Flame | 1900 K beeswax taper, 22° lateral | Evening dessert and shared bites; source off-frame |
| Architectural Day | 5500 K direct skylight, 45° diagonal | Daytime dining, entryway, vase composition |
| Gallery Halogen | 3200 K narrow spotlight, 60° elevated | Fragrance, jewellery, layered-rim details |
| Grand Chandelier | 2700 K multi-point crystal source, 75° overhead | Dinner and layered settings |
| Monolith Eclipse | 4000 K slit source, 90° top-down incidence | Sculptural teaser only; insufficient alone to explain practical use |

Light-incidence angles are separate from camera elevation. A source angle does not silently alter the camera.

### Universal negative block

No Tikli, decorative patterns, floral etching, engravings, stamped marks, factory debossing, logos on the object, compartments, altered rim, warped silhouette, plastic sheen, oversaturated colors, fingerprints, implausible reflections, floating food, intersecting props, extra utensils, invented accessories, graphic sparkle, or unsupported performance claims.

## 8. Merchandising and editorial order

Lead the main product story with dining and the layered setting once released. Follow with dessert or shared food. Introduce jewellery and fragrance farther down the page or in a separate “Beyond the table” editorial.

Suggested initial creative sequence, not a research-proven performance ranking:

1. Dinner for four: make The Host Set and piece count clear.
2. Layered setting: demonstrate compatibility with an actual tested ceramic piece.
3. Dessert close-up: demonstrate the relationship between food and reflection.
4. Jewellery ritual: introduce a quiet secondary use.
5. Fragrance arrangement: extend the visual language to the dresser.

Do not imply that a four-piece set is necessary for a single display use. Do not describe any use as most popular without observed evidence. Keep prices and delivery promises in the current offer source rather than this document.

## 9. Validation and feedback checklist

For every proposed use, record:

| Field | Required entry |
| --- | --- |
| Use-case ID | UC01–UC10 |
| Product identity | Actual SKU, sample/lot, and specification revision |
| Setup | Supporting surface, props, measured footprints, and load where relevant |
| Conditions | Contact time, temperature, moisture, and handling where relevant |
| Evidence | Photographs, observations, applicable reports, and care instructions |
| Result | Approved, revise, or hold, with reason |
| Publication scope | Exact allowed use, claims, images, and limitations |
| Owner and date | Named reviewer and decision date |

Review image comprehension with target customers: Can they identify the product, its intended use, and what is included? Ask which arrangement fits their actual home and what practical question remains. Treat responses as feedback, not representative market findings.

For published tests, track qualified product visits, offer selection, purchase conversion, and use-related questions. Compare saves separately from purchasing behavior. Review returns or complaints for expectation mismatches about marks, size, accessories, or compatibility.
