# Maison Glint — Verbal Identity & Editorial Tone

## 1. Editorial Position

Maison Glint observes the table. Light crosses steel. Linen softens the setting. People gather.

The voice carries quiet assurance. It gives the reader space. Value emerges through precise details, material contrast, and the atmosphere of a shared evening.

Write for the selected Millennial Host: someone who notices proportion, collects hosting ideas, and wants their home to feel considered. The tone is warm enough for a dinner invitation and restrained enough for a design monograph.

This file governs verbal identity. Where earlier `DESIGN.md` sample copy conflicts with these nomenclature or syntax rules, this file takes precedence for new copy. Visual direction and verified product facts remain governed by their respective sources. This file does not modify those sources.

## 2. Voice Principles

| Principle | Editorial behavior |
| --- | --- |
| Observational | Describe a visible detail or a concrete fact. |
| Declarative | State the thought. Avoid rhetorical questions and persuasive flourishes. |
| Restrained | Use one image at a time. Leave adjectives out when the noun is sufficient. |
| Domestic | Keep people, meals, linen, and gathering present. Avoid a cold laboratory voice. |
| Assured | Present the object without demanding approval or immediate action. |
| Precise | Keep quantities, prices, care, and delivery information explicit. |

**Passive confidence** describes the attitude, not a requirement to use grammatical passive voice. “The surface catches candlelight” is appropriate. Customer service still names responsibility: “We have corrected the address.”

## 3. Nomenclature Rules

### Approved vocabulary

| Term | Defined use | Example or constraint |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Object 01** | Canonical product name. Preserve the space and leading zero. | “Object 01 rests against folded linen.” |
| **Specimen** | An individual object in product or edition editorial. Use sparingly. | “One Specimen. A setting for one.” Do not imply numbering or certification. |
| **Monograph** | A substantive editorial study or named collection of notes. | “Monograph 01 — The evening table.” Do not call a receipt a Monograph. |
| **Acquisition** | The act or record of obtaining an object. Best in considered editorial contexts. | “Your Acquisition is confirmed.” Use only after actual confirmation. |
| **Chromeware** | The brand’s aesthetic category. | It does not assert chromium plating, an alloy grade, or a manufacturing process. |
| **Atelier** | The brand’s creative practice or an accurately described studio. | Do not imply an owned workshop, hand production, or a location without evidence. |
| **Mirror Polish** | Approved finish nomenclature. | It does not establish scratch resistance, optical grade, or a specific polishing method. |

Use canonical capitalization for these named terms. They are an approved vocabulary, not a quota. A short caption usually needs only Object 01 and one material observation.

Retain **The Host Set** and **The Duo** as offer names. Supporting language includes table, setting, gathering, steel, stainless steel, linen, reflection, surface, rim, candlelight, dinnerware, order, delivery, care, and return.

At first product introduction, use a clear category descriptor when needed:

> Object 01. Stainless steel dinnerware with a Mirror Polish finish.

Use material and finish descriptions only when they match the actual product. Keep quantities unambiguous:

> The Host Set contains four Object 01 pieces.

> The Duo contains two Object 01 pieces.

### Banned vocabulary

The following terms must not appear in brand-authored customer copy:

| Banned term | Editorial treatment |
| --- | --- |
| Thali | Use Object 01 or an accurate approved category description. |
| Dish | Use Object 01, dinnerware, or setting as context requires. |
| Plate | Use Object 01 or piece when counting set contents. |
| Sale | State the current price without promotional framing. |
| Discount | State the total set price. Avoid bargain positioning. |
| Hurry | Remove urgency. State a verified date or availability fact if relevant. |
| Cheap | Remove price-status comparisons. Describe material or form. |
| Modern Kitchenware | Use Chromeware or stainless steel dinnerware when accurate. |
| Buy Now | Use a clear, restrained action label. |

Apply the ban case-insensitively. Include obvious plurals, inflections, and spacing or hyphen variants. Match words rather than substrings so unrelated words are not falsely rejected. Do not evade the rules through hashtags, image text, SEO metadata, emojis, or spelling changes.

These terms appear here only to define the internal prohibition. Do not reproduce the blacklist in generated customer copy. Preserve original customer messages and factual records rather than rewriting them as brand prose.

### Generic phrasing to remove

Avoid “elevate your everyday,” “transform your space,” “where luxury meets function,” “timeless elegance,” “must-have,” “game-changer,” “perfect for every occasion,” and “you deserve.”

Avoid unsupported status claims such as “iconic,” “investment piece,” or “heirloom.” Replace praise with an observable detail. Do not replace one cliché with a more elaborate one.

## 4. Syntax & Punctuation

- Use zero exclamation marks, including full-width equivalents.
- Write short, observational, declarative sentences.
- Aim for 4–12 words per editorial sentence. Prefer no more than 18. Practical instructions may be longer when splitting them would reduce clarity.
- Keep each sentence to one main thought.
- Use paragraphs of one to three sentences. Let spacing establish pace.
- Short noun phrases are permitted in titles, captions, labels, and product identifiers.
- Use full stops and ordinary commas. Avoid ellipses, rhetorical questions, stacked dashes, and decorative punctuation.
- Avoid emojis, all-capital emphasis, repeated adjectives, and countdown language.
- Use second person chiefly for useful information: “Your order has shipped.”
- Use first person for actual responsibility. Never imply personal founder involvement in an automated action.
- Clear action labels and necessary service instructions may use imperatives. Avoid imperative-led campaign headlines.

Preferred headline:

> The table is set for four.

Preferred observation:

> Candlelight moves across the Mirror Polish surface.

Preferred factual statement:

> The Host Set contains four Object 01 pieces.

## 5. Editorial Themes

| Theme | What to observe |
| --- | --- |
| The Art of Gathering | An evening, a setting, the space between guests. |
| Material contrast | Reflective steel against linen, oak, or stone. |
| Light | A rim catching daylight or a surface reflecting a candle. |
| Use | Preparing the table, serving a meal, clearing a setting. |
| Surface over time | Actual wear, stated plainly and supported by product evidence. |

Lead with hosting. The object belongs in a home before it belongs in a collection. References to sculpture, specimens, and acquisition should support that feeling without making the customer feel examined or excluded.

## 6. Channel Rules

| Channel | Form | Editorial requirement |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Website hero | One short headline and one supporting sentence | Establish atmosphere and identify the offer nearby. |
| Product description | Two to four short sentences | Name the object, intended use, and verified characteristics. |
| Instagram caption | Two to four short lines | One scene, one observation, optional factual product identifier. |
| Pinterest | Searchable title and one to three sentences | Use accurate hosting terms. Match the linked story or offer. |
| Editorial email | Short subject, two to four compact paragraphs | Develop one observation. Include at most one main action. |
| Transactional email | Status first, then necessary details | Include order reference, quantity, and verified next steps. |
| Support reply | Answer or acknowledgement first | Own the issue. State the resolution or next update clearly. |
| Packaging | Product identifier and minimal editorial | Keep care and contents legible and factual. |

Hashtags are optional. Use at most three relevant tags, on a separate line. Avoid engagement requests such as tagging friends, commenting a keyword, or manufactured debate.

## 7. Actions & Commercial Clarity

Use an action label that describes its destination:

| Intent | Approved label |
| --- | --- |
| Product details | View Object 01 |
| Lead offer | Explore the Host Set |
| Editorial | Read the Monograph |
| Add selected offer | Add to bag |
| Checkout | Continue to checkout |
| Order status | Track your order |
| Genuine availability list | Join the waitlist |

“Acquisition” must not make a payment control ambiguous. Present the selected quantity, currency, and total beside the action. A waitlist is not a paid reservation.

Use only current, approved prices. Earlier strategy prices are planning inputs until confirmed. No invented reductions, stock pressure, edition counts, demand claims, or delivery promises.

If stock is unavailable, state it plainly:

> Object 01 is currently unavailable.

If a future release date is verified:

> The next release opens on [confirmed date].

Bracketed fields are drafting placeholders. Resolve them before publication.

## 8. Correspondence: The Zero-Sign-Off Rule

End the message after its final substantive sentence. Follow it with a blank line and the founder hallmark in bold. End there.

Do not add “Best,” “Regards,” “Warmly,” “With appreciation,” a closing thank-you, a slogan, a postscript, or an invitation to reply after the hallmark. A necessary instruction or contact route belongs in the body.

The hallmark identifies the sender. It is not a conventional farewell and must not become a decorative signature block.

**Canonical founder-authored format:**

```text
[Final substantive sentence.]

**[Approved founder name]**
**Founder, Maison Glint**
```

The founder’s preferred public name and exact hallmark have not been supplied for this document. Keep this field configurable. Do not infer initials, a handwritten mark, a founding date, or a signature from account metadata. Never send an unresolved placeholder.

Use the founder hallmark only for correspondence genuinely authored or approved by the founder. Automated and team messages use the same abrupt ending with **Maison Glint** as the truthful sender hallmark. Do not invent founder authorship.

In rich-text email, render the hallmark as actual bold text. Markdown asterisks are authoring notation. For a plain-text email, keep the two-line wording without visible formatting characters.

Required system footers and unsubscribe controls sit outside the authored message. They are not additional editorial sign-offs. Social captions, product text, and packaging copy do not need a correspondence hallmark.

These are writing examples, not evidence of live inventory or completed actions. Use transactional examples only when the stated event has occurred.

>
> The Host Set contains four Object 01 pieces.
>
> Explore the Host Set
>

### Dispatch email

**Subject:** Your order has shipped

**Body:**

> Your order [order reference] has shipped.
>
> Contents: [confirmed item and quantity].
> Estimated arrival: [verified delivery window].
> Tracking: [tracking link].
>
> **Maison Glint**

### Founder-approved delay reply

**Subject:** An update on order [order reference]

**Body:**

> Your order is delayed. I am sorry for the disruption.
>
> [Verified cause in one clear sentence.]
> The revised estimate is [verified estimate].
> I will send the next update by [confirmed date].
>
> **[Approved founder name]**
> **Founder, Maison Glint**

If no revised estimate exists, state that directly. Do not fill the gap with an invented date. The promised update date must be operationally achievable.

### Care note

Use the following only when supported by actual samples and care guidance:

> Fine hairlines can develop with use.
> They change the appearance of the Mirror Polish surface.
> Care guidance is included with your order.

Do not promise an included insert unless it is supplied. “Living patina” may support the observation, but must not replace the clear disclosure of surface wear.

## 11. Editorial Quality Gate

Before release, check the rendered customer copy, including subjects, preheaders, buttons, hashtags, and image overlays:

1. No exclamation marks or prohibited vocabulary, including obvious variants.
2. Object 01 and approved offer names remain consistent.
3. Sentences are short, concrete, and predominantly declarative.
4. No generic praise, artificial urgency, or stacked luxury terminology.
5. Material, quantity, price, delivery, and care statements match the supplied facts.
6. The text feels appropriate to a warm gathering and its actual channel.
7. Any action clearly communicates what happens next.
8. Correspondence ends with the authorized bold hallmark and no farewell.
9. No unresolved placeholders remain in publishable output.

A draft passes when it is both recognizable as Maison Glint and immediately understandable to its reader.
